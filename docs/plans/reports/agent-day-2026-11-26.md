# One agent's working day on Athena: what it cost and where it stuck

Date: 2026-11-26. Baseline: `13585e5` (`main` after Wave 10 of
[the in-use specification](../ATHENA_IN_USE_SPEC.md)). Method: the lead ran
`athena-demo` on loopback and drove `athena-mcp` over stdio, the way an agent
harness does, as two agents:

- **Sol**, the demo's seat, on `seat+knowledge`;
- **Nova**, a conductor onboarded with `POST /users/onboard_agent` and
  `profile: conductor`, with scopes `read` and `issue:write`.

Every call, its arguments and its answer were logged with byte counts. This
report gives the measured facts the
[agent's-day specification](../AGENT_DAY_SPEC.md) builds on. Finding IDs are
`DAY-n`.

## The session that was run

1. Sol boots: `my_desk`, then `current_run`.
2. Sol answers the operator's steer: `acknowledge_run_control`, then
   `complete_run_control`.
3. Sol finishes the claim it holds: it reads the work context, heartbeats,
   comments, completes the claim, and asks to close the issue, which is gated
   by an approval.
4. Nova creates an outcome with a work order, adds one slice under it, and
   delegates the slice to Sol.
5. Sol picks the slice up with `claim_next_ready` and yields it with a blocking
   question.
6. Nova answers with a comment.
7. Sol re-claims the slice, resumes the handoff, completes the claim and asks
   to close the issue. It long-polls `wait_for_events` until the operator
   approves, then replays the close with the same idempotency key.

Sol made 37 tool calls; Nova made 18. Every step ended where the protocol says
it should.

## What worked

- **Refusals carry the next move.** A stale `if_match` answered 412 with
  `current_etag` and `next: get_issue`. A gated close answered
  `approval_required` with `next: wait_for_events`, including the arguments to
  pass. `wait_for_events` returned 5.0 seconds after the operator approved, and
  the retry under the same key answered with `_replayed: true`.
- **Recovery after a yield is in one answer.** `claim_next_ready` returned the
  open handoff together with the lease, so resuming needed no extra read.
- `complete_claim` says plainly that it did not close the issue.

## Findings

**DAY-1. Every session and every desk read is heavy, and much of it repeats.**
- Before its first call, a session reads the tool listing and the server
  instructions:

  | Profile | Tools | Listing bytes | Instructions bytes |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | `seat+knowledge` | 40 | 62,758 | 1,836 |
  | `conductor` | 42 | 67,165 | 1,836 |

- The largest tool definitions are `complete_claim` (5,452 bytes),
  `claim_issue` (4,343), `wait_for_events` (3,100), `update_issue` (3,067) and
  `claim_next_ready` (2,838).
- A full `my_desk` answered 7,794 bytes, of which 3,560 (46%) was fixed
  explanatory text identical on every call: `meaning` strings, `semantics`, and
  `protocol`, which appears twice (under `work` and under `office`).
- A full work-context packet answered 7,545 bytes, of which 1,284 was fixed
  text and 1,242 was keys whose value was `null`. Each `recent_activity` item
  carries six fields that were null on every native event, and wraps an
  11-character `detail` in `{text, total_chars, truncated}`.

**DAY-2. The desk lists everything and ranks nothing.**
- Sol's first desk held three things to act on: an unanswered operator steer,
  a held claim 29 minutes from expiry, and an approval the operator had not yet
  decided.
- They were in three different sections (`asks.run_controls`, `work.leases`,
  `work.waiting_on_operator`) among 7.8 KB.
- Nothing said which came first or which call to make.

**DAY-3. A delegate's question does not reach the account that delegated the
work.**
- Sol yielded the slice Nova had created and delegated, with
  `reason: needs_input` and a blocking question.
- The question appeared on Sol's desk (`waiting_on_operator`) and in Nova's
  `get_attention_ranking`, but not on Nova's desk.
- Nova's `waiting_on` lane covers only delegations that have gone 24 hours
  without pickup.
- The attention row's `next_action` was `operator-link` to `/aegis/issues/5`, a
  page an agent cannot use. The move that answers it is `comment_on_issue`.

**DAY-4. The answer does not travel with the question.**
- When Sol re-claimed the slice, the open handoff came back but Nova's answering
  comment did not.
- Sol needed `list_issue_comments` to read the answer to its own question.

**DAY-5. A slice does not show its parent's contract.**
- The outcome's body carried `## Goal` and `## Success criteria`.
- The slice's work-context packet showed only the parent's title and status
  under `hierarchy.parent`.
- `work_order` reported all six parts missing.
- An agent working the slice is not shown the acceptance criteria it is meant
  to meet.

**DAY-6. The work context does not know the caller holds the lease.**
- After Sol claimed, the packet still said
  `how_to_claim.next_operation: "get_issue_lease then claim_issue"`, and it
  carried no lease, generation or expiry.
- Every `complete_claim` and `yield_claim` in the session was preceded by a
  `get_issue_lease` call made only to read the generation: four calls.

**DAY-7. Argument shapes are inconsistent and their refusals do not show the
expected shape.**
- `evidence` is a list of strings on `yield_claim` and a list of
  `{kind, source, ref, scope}` objects on `complete_claim`.
- The item shape of `results` exists only in the argument's description; the
  schema says `{type: object, additionalProperties: true}`.
- Both wrong guesses were refused with `invalid_arguments` and text such as
  "evidence: Input should be a valid list", with no expected shape and no
  `next`. Two calls were wasted.
- `complete_claim`'s evidence kinds (`event`, `handoff`, `doc`, `file`,
  `manifest`, `attachment`, `forge_event`) have none for the most common
  evidence a builder reports: a command it ran and what it printed.

**DAY-8. A returning agent starts a new run every time.**
- Each `athena-mcp` process minted a new `mcp-<hex>` run. Sol's work spanned
  the demo's seeded run plus one new run for each of the ten processes, and the
  claim, the yield and the resume of one slice each landed on a different
  run.
- `ATHENA_RUN_ID` exists and is documented in `OPERATIONS.md` and
  `CONDUCTOR_RECIPE.md`, but not in the boot block or `RUNTIME_RECIPE.md`, and
  no config Athena prints sets it.
- The desk does not tell an agent which run it last wrote under, and
  `begin_run` is in no seat profile.

**DAY-9. Some ids cannot be discovered from the profile that needs them.**
- The `conductor` profile has no `list_projects`; the recipe has the operator
  supply the project id.
- `assign_issue_to_seat` takes a seat slug, but a non-admin conductor has no
  read that lists slugs. Nova's call answered "unknown seat 'sol'".
- `seat+knowledge` has `list_pages(space_id)` and no `list_spaces`.

**DAY-10. A conductor's notifications are one row per event.**
- After one slice was closed, Nova had 9 unread notifications. Most were
  approval bookkeeping on that one issue: `approval_requested`,
  `approval_approved`, `approval_consumed` and `changed_status`.
- Nothing told Nova that every slice of the outcome was now done.

**DAY-11. Smaller.**
- `complete_claim` and the closing `update_issue(status=...)` were always made
  together.
- The approval decision takes `approve` while the state it reports is
  `approved`; the first operator call used the state's spelling and was
  refused.
- `get_idempotency_key` reports `unused` for a key whose call was refused with
  `approval_required`. This is accurate, but it reads as though the call was
  never made.
